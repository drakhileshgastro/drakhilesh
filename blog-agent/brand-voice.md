## 4. Voice & Tone

### The Brand Personality
Think of Dr. Akhilesh as a **trusted family doctor with super-specialist training** — the warmth of a known physician, the authority of a DM gastroenterologist.

| Attribute | Description |
|---|---|
| Warm | "Ghabrana mat..." — reassures before explaining |
| Expert | Uses precise medical terms + explains them immediately |
| Local | Ranchi-specific, Jharkhand-aware, local food context |
| Patient | Never condescending — always explains in simple terms |
| Honest | Says when something is serious, says when it is not |

### Voice in Practice

**DO say:**
- "Ranchi ke kai patients ko fatty liver ki problem ho rahi hai — lekin sahi jaankari se isey manage kiya ja sakta hai."
- "Dr. Akhilesh Yadav kehte hain: 'Fatty liver ke early stage mein diet aur exercise se kaafi farak padta hai.'"
- "Aapko agar ye symptoms dikh rahein hain toh der mat karein — ek baar specialist se milein."
- "Ghabrana mat — yeh common condition hai aur treatable hai."

**DON'T say:**
- "In today's digital age, liver disease is a growing concern..."
- "Namaskar doston, aaj hum baat karenge..."
- "This will definitely cure your problem..."
- "You might have [disease]..." (diagnostic claim)
- "100% painless" or "permanent cure"

## 5. Language Rules — Hinglish Style Guide

### The 70/30 Rule
- **70% Hindi** — emotions, advice, patient-facing sentences
- **30% English** — medical terms, procedure names, technical concepts

### Hindi First, English Medical Terms
```
CORRECT:
"Aapke liver mein fatty changes ho rahe hain — ise NAFLD kehte hain."

WRONG:
"Fatty liver means accumulation of fat in hepatocytes, known as NAFLD."
```

### Medical Terms That Stay in English (Always)
fatty liver, liver cirrhosis, hepatitis, endoscopy, colonoscopy, ERCP, EUS,
GERD, IBS, IBD, Crohn's disease, jaundice, bilirubin, SGOT, SGPT, AFP,
DM Gastroenterology, gastroenterologist, hepatologist, H. Pylori, LFT

### Patient vs Medical Vocabulary
| Patient says | We write |
|---|---|
| पेट में जलन | acidity / GERD |
| पीलिया | jaundice (NEVER "pilia") |
| खून की उल्टी | hematemesis / khoon ki ulti |
| कब्ज | kabj / constipation |
| गैस | gas / bloating |
| पित्त की थैली | gallbladder |

### FORBIDDEN WORDS — Never Use
- "pilia" — always say "jaundice"
- "100% cure" / "permanent cure" / "definitely cure" / "guaranteed results"
- "painless procedure" — say "sedation diya jaata hai, minimal discomfort"
- "best doctor in India" (unverifiable superlative)
- Any dosage: "1 tablet twice daily" / "500mg" — never
- "You have [disease]" — diagnostic claim — never
- "In today's digital age..." — AI-sounding opener
- "Namaskar doston..." — generic video-style opener

## 6. Local Food & Cultural References

Use Jharkhand/Bihar food references to make content feel local and credible:

### Good for Liver/Gut (recommend)
- **Sattu ka paani** — high protein, low glycemic — safe for fatty liver patients
- **Chhachh (buttermilk)** — probiotic, low fat — good for gut health
- **Papaya** — liver-friendly, digestive enzyme (papain)
- **Bajra roti / jowar roti** — high fiber, low glycemic index
- **Lauki (bottle gourd)** — light, easy on digestion
- **Karela (bitter gourd)** — blood sugar, mild liver support

### Caution / Limit (advise moderation)
- **Litti-chokha** — traditional but ghee-heavy; advise baked litti, less ghee
- **Mahua (tribal areas)** — alcohol-related liver disease risk — mention sensitively
- **Pakoda / jalebi** — deep-fried / high sugar — avoid in fatty liver
- **Namkeen / achaar** — high sodium — bad for ascites patients

### Regional Patient Geography
- **Deoghar/Dumka** — religious pilgrims, waterborne hepatitis A/E risk
- **Mining belt (Dhanbad, Bokaro)** — industrial workers, alcohol liver disease
- **Tribal areas (Palamu, Khunti)** — kala-azar, indigenous food practices
- **Gaya (Bihar)** — neighboring patients; Bihar border crossover
